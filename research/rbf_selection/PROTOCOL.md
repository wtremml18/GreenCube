# Frozen primary comparison (before downstream training outcomes)

Date: 2026-10-18. Hypothesis: block orthogonal least-squares selection through an image-observation linearization can allocate a compact divergence-free velocity basis more effectively than spatial coverage at a fixed number of kernels. This is an exploratory extension of thesis Sections 2.4–2.6, not a global optimality claim.

All primary fits use the same four ScalarSyn frames, four training cameras, reserved fifth camera, initial image-fitted Gaussian geometry, sixteen fixed sensor locations and identical noisy readings from the preceding study. Each selected kernel has three coefficient components. All centers, radii and weights are subsequently trainable under the same upstream objective, 200 epochs and learning-rate schedule. The initial coefficients are zero for every method. No velocity truth or held-out camera enters basis selection.

The candidate dictionary uses 192 farthest-spaced initial Gaussian centers inside the four training-camera silhouettes, with radii 30, 60 and 120 native voxels: 576 vector blocks. This selection changes motion RBFs, not appearance Gaussians or sensors. The initial pilot renderer finite-difference Jacobian uses initial geometry, a one-step displacement, and 8x8 pooled grayscale training images. Targets are consecutive training-image differences for the three intervals. This is a local surrogate that omits evolving geometry, emission and appearance updates; final fitting uses the original nonlinear pipeline including inflow.

Block OLS greedily selects whole three-component basis blocks by reduction in residual sum of squares after projection against existing blocks. It jointly scores three independent interval coefficient vectors sharing a spatial basis. Image rows and the 48 sensor-component rows are each normalized by their square-root count; the joint selector uses sensor weight one. Selection budgets are 32, 64 and 128. A numerical rank threshold prevents exact redundant columns from destabilizing the selection.

Controls: image-only OLS; joint image/sensor OLS; random blocks from the identical dictionary (seed 618); farthest coverage through the same center pool with cyclic 30/60/120 radii; domain coverage obtained by farthest subsampling the upstream Poisson sample, using the upstream radius rule for the exact selected count. That last control is a matched-count adaptation of upstream initialization, not an assertion that Poisson sampling itself returns the requested count.

Primary seed 42 runs all five methods at all three budgets. Seeds 43 and 44 repeat the three planned main methods (domain coverage, support coverage, joint OLS) at all three budgets. Image-only training controls at budgets 64 and 128 compare domain coverage, support coverage and image-only OLS with seed 42. These latter controls have no sensors in either selection or training. All completed conditions are retained.

Evaluation uses the preceding study's frozen common targets, 4,096 smoke voxels per velocity interval, excluding every earlier sensor layout by over two voxels. Held-out image PSNR and actual vector RMSE are separate outcomes. Reported motion scores average the vector RMSE of each of the three velocity frames, then average across optimization seeds where repeated; they are not a single pooled-root loss. Reserved-camera PSNR averages four frame scores. Selection/Jacobian construction time is reported separately and charged when assessing total cost; shared reuse is not silently treated as free. Comparison across kernel budgets is not by itself an equal-wall-time comparison. Any additional time-matched controls or revisions will be labelled follow-ups.

This is one synthetic scene, four frames, a shared initial geometry and development data already inspected in earlier experiments. Optimization seeds are not independent scenes. No final-test or publication novelty claim follows from a successful pilot.

## Declared follow-up after initial 32/64-kernel results

The first small-budget OLS runs did not improve motion over broad domain coverage. Added a conventional support-scale selection control at all three budgets: four-fold CV holds out whole sensor locations (and all three times together), fits a linear coefficient-ridge surrogate, and chooses a common radius multiplier from 0.5, 1 and 2 with relative ridge from 1e-4, 0.01 and 1. The resulting radius initializes the same nonlinear camera/sensor fit; its weights remain initially zero. This changes neither the sixteen readings nor final evaluation targets. It tests the thesis's parameter-selection approach separately from image-based basis selection. CV selects an initializer for a different final objective, so surrogate CV is not a guarantee of optimal end-to-end reconstruction. This is an exploratory follow-up, not part of the original frozen comparison.

A further exploratory regularized-OLS control uses the identical image dictionary and candidate locations. Its coefficient ridge strength is chosen by four-fold sensor-location CV on the full dictionary, over relative values 1e-6 through 1e2. It then performs block OLS on the image/sensor matrix augmented by the square root of that coefficient penalty. This adapts the thesis's regularized-OLS option; the coefficient penalty is only for selection, not an assertion that it matches the final nonlinear physical regularization. Budgets 32, 64 and 128 are tested with seed 42; results are labelled follow-ups. Simple support-radius CV selected the original domain-coverage radius at all three budgets, so that control reuses the identical domain-coverage fits rather than pretending identical initializers are new experiments.

## Derivative audit and corrected primary selector

After the first fifteen seed-42 fits completed, the renderer derivative audit failed: three finite-difference probes showed relative discrepancies of 6.6%, 1.7% and 55.2% against the upstream backward operator at displacement 0.01. A step-size sweep down to 0.00005 did not converge reliably. The root cause is unresolved; discrete rasterization, visibility and floating-point effects are possible explanations, not established diagnoses. The failed check remains in `results/derivative_check.json`. The six `image_ols` and `joint_ols` fits are retained as finite-difference pilots, not primary validated-selector evidence. Their nine seed-42 control fits remain applicable.

Before running repeats, image-only controls or ROLS, the image dictionary was rebuilt using renderer backward gradients with respect to Gaussian positions, composed with the analytic RBF matrix. Four independent random-probe vector-Jacobian products agree with the assembled matrix to relative error below 4.5e-7. This establishes agreement with the upstream optimizer's backward operator; it does not resolve the finite-difference failure or validate the full temporal objective. Four kernel parameter derivative checks pass their original 5% tolerance; the analytic matrix matches the upstream GPU kernel within 1.15e-6 absolute error.

The backward matrix differs from the finite-difference matrix by 22.1% in Frobenius norm. Corrected selectors are explicitly named `image_adols` and `joint_adols`. Both are run at all three budgets with seed 42; `joint_adols` replaces `joint_ols` in the planned seed-43/44 repeats, and `image_adols` replaces `image_ols` in image-only training controls. The ROLS follow-up uses the backward dictionary. This adds six corrected-selector fits to the original design, for 48 total fits including six finite-difference pilots. All are retained in tabular results; primary plots distinguish the corrected selectors from the failed-derivative pilots.

The corrected dictionary construction took 3.42 seconds on the recorded GPU, versus 17.30 seconds for the finite-difference dictionary. Each selector is charged its corresponding construction time plus its own selection/CV time. These are incremental measurements with shared initial appearance geometry, not full pipeline timing.

## Additional renderer diagnostic, declared during reconstruction repeats

After seeing nonconvergence for small finite-difference steps, a separate diagnostic will test displacements 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01, 0.005 and 0.002 with float64 accumulation of the scalar image probes. Rendering and positions still use upstream float32. The diagnostic reports the original 5% relative-error tolerance at the preselected step 0.05 and the entire sweep; it preserves the original failed check. This tests sensitivity to step size/probe accumulation, not a replacement method or a changed pass threshold for the original experiment. It runs after timed reconstruction to avoid GPU contention.

## Follow-up after all initial 48 fits and their common-point audit

Regularized OLS improves common-point motion RMSE over broad domain coverage at all three budgets (seed 42); at 64 kernels the scores are 0.5670 and 0.5968. To check optimization stability without selecting only a favorable budget, all three regularized budgets are repeated at seeds 43 and 44. This adds six fits, for 54 total. These are explicitly post-result follow-ups, not additional independent scenes.

A CPU selection ablation removes the image residual/Jacobian but retains image-derived candidate geometry and the same sensor-selected ridge. It selects the identical kernel sets at all three budgets (the same order at 32; different orders at 64/128). Thus this experiment supplies no evidence that the image Jacobian improves the winning selection. No duplicate reconstruction fits are counted for an unchanged basis set. The image geometry itself has not been removed by this ablation.

A direct linear sensor-only control is also added, using the same image-derived full candidate bank and the already selected four-fold sensor-CV coefficient ridge. This tests whether direct regression in the same feature family can explain the apparent Gaussian reconstruction benefit. No new truth-based parameter search is permitted. Evaluation uses the same frozen common targets, with no cameras in coefficient fitting. Selected-subset versions use the same fixed ridge and report their limitations separately.

The direct sensor-only control also reports the existing domain-coverage support-CV initializers using their already chosen ridge values. There are seven direct linear fits (full bank, three selected subsets, three domain-CV bases), evaluated at three frames each. Selected subsets keep the full-bank absolute coefficient prior; they do not receive a new subset-specific hyperparameter search. These controls are separate from the 54 nonlinear camera fits.

Before concluding that image-derived candidate geometry helps, three additional direct-regression controls use 192 farthest-spaced centers from uniform domain pools (4,096 candidates, seeds 914–916), the same coordinate bounds as the image-supported pool, and the same three support radii. Each 576-block bank chooses its ridge by the same four sensor-location folds and nine-value grid. No reference velocity or density is used to generate centers or tune the fit. These add three linear fits, for ten linear fits and thirty frame rows, without adding camera reconstructions. Candidate-pool repeats remain within the same scene.
